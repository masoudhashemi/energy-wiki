Concept Learning and LLM Wikis with Energy-Based Models

A mathematical framework for turning messy knowledge bases into connected wikis and generating new explanations using energy-based models and textual gradients.
Masoud Hashemi · April 2026 · Code & Repository (GitHub)

I like to have a mathematical formulation for agentic tasks and iterative improvement loops. A clear math formulation helps us define the loops accurately, separate the tasks, and set clear boundaries for how different parts of the system interact. Instead of treating agentic workflows as ad-hoc heuristics, we can pose them as well-defined optimizations performed during the execution lifetime of the agent.

In this post, we apply Concept Learning with Energy-Based Models (Mordatch, 2018) to the problem of building an LLM Wiki from a messy knowledge base.

What is an LLM Wiki and Why Use It?

Consider a simple query-time retrieval baseline: it searches isolated text chunks and generates an answer without maintaining a compiled wiki. Useful connections must be reconstructed for each query. A persistent wiki offers another way to organize that work: capture relationships once, then make them available to subsequent readers and agents.

An LLM Wiki takes the opposite approach. Instead of waiting for a query to read raw files, an agent continuously compiles raw notes, papers, and logs into a structured, interlinked web of wiki pages. Shared ideas are consolidated into concept pages, related documents are connected with bidirectional links, conflicting claims between notes are reconciled in place, and irrelevant clutter is filtered out. The result is a living knowledge graph that compounds understanding over time and can be browsed directly by both humans and agents.

Following Mordatch's framework, a concept in this wiki is defined by a single energy function $E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w})$ over entities $\mathbf{x}$ (raw documents $\mathbf{x}^0$ and compiled wiki pages $\mathbf{x}^1$), an attention mask $\mathbf{a}$ over participating entities, and a concept code $\mathbf{w}$. Zero-energy configurations imply that the entities selected by the attention mask satisfy the concept.

Why an Energy-Based Formulation Matters: A shared energy function $E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w})$ lets us express finding concepts (changing $\mathbf{w}$), selecting documents (changing $\mathbf{a}$), compiling wiki pages (changing $\mathbf{x}^1$), and generating new synthetic documents through one formulation. Each operation fixes the other two variables. Textual gradients—natural-language critiques—provide actionable feedback for revising the chosen variable under those fixed conditions.

The shared energy supports three conditional operations: infer a concept from fixed documents and membership, identify documents under a fixed concept, or synthesize text under fixed concepts and membership. These are distinct optimizations over one variable at a time. The symbolic implementation evaluates a hand-designed energy, while the live demonstrations use LLM critiques and scores. Learning the energy parameters is a further research step.

1. Energy-Based Concept Models for Knowledge Bases

In a knowledge base, an event is a transition from an initial state of $N$ raw documents $\mathbf{x}^0 = [\mathbf{x}_1^0, \dots, \mathbf{x}_N^0]$ to a compiled wiki state $\mathbf{x}^1 = [\mathbf{x}_1^1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_N^1]$. Attention over the $N$ documents is represented by a continuous mask $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^N$, where each gate $\sigma(\mathbf{a}_i) \in (0, 1)$ measures how strongly document $i$ participates in the concept.

A concept is defined by a non-negative energy function $E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w}) \ge 0$, where $\mathbf{w}$ is a natural-language concept code. When the documents selected by $\mathbf{a}$ form a coherent, well-linked concept under $\mathbf{w}$, the energy is zero ($E_\theta = 0$). When unrelated documents are included, relevant documents are missing, or shared ideas are left unlinked, the energy is positive ($E_\theta > 0$).

In the proposed probabilistic formulation, conditional distributions over wiki states and attention masks can be defined through a normalizable Boltzmann model:

$$p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w}) \propto \exp\{-E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w})\}, \qquad p(\mathbf{a} \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{w}) \propto \exp\{-E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w})\}$$

To capture how documents relate to one another rather than scoring each document in isolation, the energy function uses a Relation Network architecture that evaluates pairs of documents $(i, j)$ gated by their attention weights:

$$E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w}) = f_\theta\left( \sum_{t, i, j} \sigma(\mathbf{a}_i)\sigma(\mathbf{a}_j) \cdot g_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i^t, \mathbf{x}_j^t, \mathbf{w}), \, \mathbf{w} \right)^2$$

Here $g_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j, \mathbf{w})$ is a pairwise critic that inspects two documents together. It checks whether they share the same underlying mechanism, whether they contradict each other, and whether their compiled wiki pages are connected by a link.

To connect this framework to text editing, we propose the following textual analogue of an SGLD update, where $\nabla^{\text{text}} E_\theta$ denotes natural-language critiques and edit instructions. This is schematic notation: subtraction, step size, and noise on text are not numerical Langevin operations in the current implementation.

$$\mathbf{x}^k = \mathbf{x}^{k-1} - \frac{\alpha}{2} \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}^{\text{text}} E_\theta(\mathbf{x}^{k-1}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w}) + \omega^k, \qquad \mathbf{a}^k = \mathbf{a}^{k-1} - \frac{\alpha}{2} \nabla_{\mathbf{a}}^{\text{text}} E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}^{k-1}, \mathbf{w}) + \omega^k$$

Operationally, the live loop proposes a revised state from a critique, evaluates it, and applies a stated acceptance rule. This preserves the energy-guided design without assuming that stochastic LLM edits sample a Boltzmann distribution. 2. Definitions and How Entities Relate

In a real knowledge base, documents are messy and cover overlapping topics. Because each concept $\mathbf{w}^{(m)}$ has its own independent sigmoid gate $\sigma(\mathbf{a}_i^{(m)}) \in (0, 1)$, there is no one-concept-per-document assumption. A single document can belong to zero concepts (unrelated noise), one concept (a specialized note), or several concepts at once (a bridge paper that connects two fields).

Why Pairwise Relations Matter: Pointwise Search vs. Relation Network

Mordatch's relation-network experiments motivate asking whether explicit document relationships help wiki construction. Our symbolic example explores this mechanism with a keyword-based unary baseline. A controlled comparison with ordinary LLM revision is needed to assess its benefit in live wiki compilation.

Energy-Based Sampling and Critique-Guided Text Editing

Energy scores and sampling

An energy function \(E(x,w)\) scores a state \(x\) under parameters or concept \(w\); it does not, by itself, generate states. In a Boltzmann model, the score defines a probability distribution, \(p(x\mid w)=\exp(-E(x,w))/Z(w)\). For a fixed model, lower-energy states receive greater probability, while the partition function \(Z\) normalizes the distribution. [[Maximum Entropy Inverse Reinforcement Learning]]

A sampler is a separate procedure for drawing states from that distribution. Hinton’s contrastive-divergence method uses a short MCMC or Langevin chain to produce negative samples and compares their energies with data states. [[Training Products of Experts by Minimizing Contrastive Divergence]] Welling and Teh describe SGLD, which adds calibrated noise to gradient updates and, under its stated limiting conditions, samples from the target distribution. [[Bayesian Learning via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics]] Mordatch et al. use SGLD at execution time to optimize entity states or attention under an energy model. [[Concept Learning with Energy-Based Models]]

These sampling accounts are not entirely aligned: the contradiction note reports that its K=10 chains fail to reach low-energy modes without a sampler-KL objective, contrary to the assumption that short chains mix sufficiently. [[Lab Notebook #42: Why Short-Run K=10 Langevin Chains Fail Without KL Shaping]] Thus, an energy score and a method for sampling from its distribution are distinct, and short-run sampling may be inadequate.

Critique-guided text editing

A separate proposed application is iterative editing of discrete text. At execution time, a critic diagnoses failures and returns natural-language edit instructions; those instructions guide repeated revisions to a prompt or document. ProTeGi uses this approach to edit instructions based on failure minibatches. [[ProTeGi: Prompt Optimization with Textual Gradients]] TextGrad generalizes textual gradients to discrete variables such as prompts, code, and wiki pages, using a loss or energy rubric to guide successive edits. [[TextGrad: Automatic Differentiation via Text]]

For wiki compilation, the devlog describes pairwise relation critiques aimed at missing cross-document links, rather than only unary critiques of individual pages. [[DevLog 2026-11-12: Redis Cache Timeout & Pairwise Wiki-Link Textual Gradients]] This is language-based guidance for discrete edits—not numerical gradient updates or Langevin sampling. The energy-model sources explain scoring and sampling; they do not establish that critique-guided text edits are Langevin dynamics.

6. What the Current Experiments Establish

The symbolic suites implement hand-designed energy evaluation and updates; the original live wiki suite demonstrates concept proposals and compilation with constructed intermediate traces; the bridge suite demonstrates two live revisions. The older “with/without KL” comparison changes step size and noise, and “trained on” conditions use manually selected weights. Those are configuration studies. Energy-guided synthesis remains the research direction. These small constructed corpora do not yet establish learned energy parameters, Langevin mixing, or superior wiki quality. Relevant conflicting sources should remain available; differences in assumptions or asymptotic versus finite-step regimes should not be treated automatically as contradictions. Next evaluations should use held-out corpora, repeated runs, independently assessed source support and coverage, useful links, contradiction handling, and downstream question answering. Fresh-context ratings by the same model are informative diagnostics, not independent validation.
