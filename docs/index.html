Concept Learning and LLM Wikis with Energy-Based Models

A mathematical framework for turning messy knowledge bases into connected wikis and generating new knowledge using energy-based models and textual gradients.
Masoud Hashemi · April 2026

I like to have a mathematical formulation for agentic tasks and iterative improvement loops. A clear math formulation helps us define the loops accurately, separate the tasks, and set clear boundaries for how different parts of the system interact. Instead of treating agentic workflows as ad-hoc heuristics, we can pose them as well-defined optimizations performed during the execution lifetime of the agent.

In this post, we apply Concept Learning with Energy-Based Models (Mordatch, 2018) to the problem of building an LLM Wiki from a messy knowledge base.

What is an LLM Wiki and Why Use It?

Standard retrieval systems treat a knowledge base as a passive pile of isolated text chunks. Every time you ask a question, the system searches for a few fragments and tries to piece together an answer from scratch. Nothing builds up over time, and deeper connections or contradictions across documents are easily missed.

An LLM Wiki takes the opposite approach. Instead of waiting for a query to read raw files, an agent continuously compiles raw notes, papers, and logs into a structured, interlinked web of wiki pages. Shared ideas are consolidated into concept pages, related documents are connected with bidirectional links, conflicting claims between notes are reconciled in place, and irrelevant clutter is filtered out. The result is a living knowledge graph that compounds understanding over time and can be browsed directly by both humans and agents.

Following Mordatch's framework, a concept in this wiki is defined by a single energy function $E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w})$ over entities $\mathbf{x}$ (raw documents $\mathbf{x}^0$ and compiled wiki pages $\mathbf{x}^1$), an attention mask $\mathbf{a}$ over participating entities, and a concept code $\mathbf{w}$. Zero-energy configurations imply that the entities selected by the attention mask satisfy the concept.

Why an Energy-Based Formulation Matters: By formulating the system around a single energy function $E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w})$ and replacing numerical gradients with textual gradients (natural-language critiques), finding concepts (optimizing over $\mathbf{w}$), selecting which documents to connect (optimizing over $\mathbf{a}$), compiling wiki pages (optimizing over $\mathbf{x}^1$), and generating brand-new data (optimizing over new synthetic documents) are all expressed with the exact same formulation and optimization loop.

1. Energy-Based Concept Models for Knowledge Bases

In a knowledge base, an event is a transition from an initial state of $N$ raw documents $\mathbf{x}^0 = [\mathbf{x}_1^0, \dots, \mathbf{x}_N^0]$ to a compiled wiki state $\mathbf{x}^1 = [\mathbf{x}_1^1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_N^1]$. Attention over the $N$ documents is represented by a continuous mask $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^N$, where each gate $\sigma(\mathbf{a}_i) \in (0, 1)$ measures how strongly document $i$ participates in the concept.

A concept is defined by a non-negative energy function $E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w}) \ge 0$, where $\mathbf{w}$ is a natural-language concept code. When the documents selected by $\mathbf{a}$ form a coherent, well-linked concept under $\mathbf{w}$, the energy is zero ($E_\theta = 0$). When unrelated documents are included, relevant documents are missing, or shared ideas are left unlinked, the energy is positive ($E_\theta > 0$).

The conditional probabilities of a wiki state $\mathbf{x}$ and an attention mask $\mathbf{a}$ follow Boltzmann distributions:

$$p(\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w}) \propto \exp\{-E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w})\}, \qquad p(\mathbf{a} \mid \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{w}) \propto \exp\{-E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w})\}$$

To capture how documents relate to one another rather than scoring each document in isolation, the energy function uses a Relation Network architecture that evaluates pairs of documents $(i, j)$ gated by their attention weights:

$$E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w}) = f_\theta\left( \sum_{t, i, j} \sigma(\mathbf{a}_i)\sigma(\mathbf{a}_j) \cdot g_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i^t, \mathbf{x}_j^t, \mathbf{w}), \, \mathbf{w} \right)^2$$

Here $g_\theta(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j, \mathbf{w})$ is a pairwise critic that inspects two documents together. It checks whether they share the same underlying mechanism, whether they contradict each other, and whether their compiled wiki pages are connected by a link.

At execution time, we minimize $E_\theta$ using $K$ steps of Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD), where the gradients $\nabla^{\text{text}} E_\theta$ are natural-language critiques and edit instructions produced by the critic:

$$\mathbf{x}^k = \mathbf{x}^{k-1} - \frac{\alpha}{2} \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}^{\text{text}} E_\theta(\mathbf{x}^{k-1}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w}) + \omega^k, \qquad \mathbf{a}^k = \mathbf{a}^{k-1} - \frac{\alpha}{2} \nabla_{\mathbf{a}}^{\text{text}} E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}^{k-1}, \mathbf{w}) + \omega^k$$

2. Definitions and How Entities Relate

In a real knowledge base, documents are messy and cover overlapping topics. Because each concept $\mathbf{w}^{(m)}$ has its own independent sigmoid gate $\sigma(\mathbf{a}_i^{(m)}) \in (0, 1)$, there is no one-concept-per-document assumption. A single document can belong to zero concepts (unrelated noise), one concept (a specialized note), or several concepts at once (a bridge paper that connects two fields).

Why Pairwise Relations Matter: Pointwise Search vs. Relation Network

In Section 5.3 of Mordatch (2018), removing the pairwise relation network causes the model to fail on concepts that require coordinating multiple entities. We observe the exact same phenomenon when building a wiki from messy text.

If we score each document in isolation (standard pointwise search), a note about Smart Grid HVAC Thermal Energy and Sensor Sampling scores high because it contains the words energy, sampling, partition, and gradient. However, when the Relation Network evaluates pairs of documents together via $\sigma(a_i)\sigma(a_j)g_\theta(x_i, x_j, \mathbf{w})$, it sees that HVAC ductwork shares no conceptual mechanism with Boltzmann distributions or Markov chains. This pairwise mismatch creates high energy, pushing the attention on the trap notes down to zero over $K = 10$ steps.

4. Generating New Data with the Same Energy Formulation

Because an energy-based model defines what a valid configuration looks like rather than learning a fixed input-to-output mapping, data generation requires no new architecture. We use the exact same energy function $E_\theta(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w})$ and the same $K$-step textual optimization loop to generate three types of new data:

  1. Generating New "Bridge" Documents by Summing Energies: In Mordatch's framework, composing two concepts corresponds to adding their energy functions: $$E_{\text{comp}}(x_{\text{new}}) = E_\theta([\mathbf{x}, x_{\text{new}}], \mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{w}^{(1)}) + E_\theta([\mathbf{x}, x_{\text{new}}], \mathbf{a}_2, \mathbf{w}^{(2)})$$ To generate a new article that bridges two separate clusters in the wiki (for example, Boltzmann Partition Sampling and Discrete Textual Gradients), we start with a rough two-sentence draft $x_{\text{new}}^{(0)}$ and run $K = 10$ steps of textual optimization. At each step, the energy critic points out what mathematical links or explanations are missing between the draft and both clusters, and the editor rewrites the draft until the composite energy approaches zero.
  2. Generating Hard-Negative Test Data: By optimizing a synthetic document to have low pointwise keyword energy (using all the right technical terms) but high pairwise relational energy (applying those terms in an incompatible field like molecular biology), the model generates realistic "hard negatives" to test whether retrieval systems truly understand the topic or merely match keywords.
  3. Re-Enacting Concepts in a New Domain: In Section 5.4 of Mordatch (2018), an energy function learned in a 2D particle world is reused directly to guide a 3D robotic arm. Similarly, we take a concept code $\mathbf{w}^*$ learned from machine learning notes and use $E_\theta(x_{\text{target}}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{w}^*)$ to generate a system design in a completely different field (distributed database consensus) that follows the exact same relational structure.

Energy objective and sampling

Let $x$ be a document and $w$ its task or rubric. An energy model assigns lower energy to documents that better satisfy the rubric, defining the Boltzmann distribution $p_\theta(x\mid w) = \exp[-E_\theta(x,w)]/Z_\theta(w)$, where $Z_\theta(w) = \int \exp[-E_\theta(x,w)]\,dx$ is the partition function. In practice, $Z_\theta(w)$ is difficult to compute. One possible approximation is importance sampling, $Z_\theta(w) \approx \frac{1}{M}\sum_m \exp[-E_\theta(x_m,w)]/r(x_m)$, for samples $x_m$ drawn from proposal $r$. This estimate can be noisy when the proposal poorly covers likely documents; how to obtain a reliable estimate, or its gradient, remains open in this draft.

A continuous relaxation can be sampled with a short-run Langevin update: x_{t+1} = x_t - (η/2) ∇_x E_θ(x_t,w) + √η ξ_t, ξ_t ~ N(0,I). For text, this update is an idealization: gradients must ultimately be translated into edits to discrete tokens or spans. The energy-model and sampling background is connected to [[Concept Learning with Energy-Based Models]] and [[Bayesian Learning via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics]].

Pairwise-gated textual gradients

A critic scores a draft against a rubric covering such properties as factual support, clarity, and coherence. Split the draft into spans $x_i$. A relation network computes $g_\theta(x_i,x_j,w)$, estimating whether spans $i$ and $j$ support, contradict, or need to be linked. Attention masks $a_i$ and $a_j$ gate each proposed relation: q_ij = σ(a_i) σ(a_j) g_θ(x_i,x_j,w). Thus a relation contributes strongly only when both spans are attended to. The critic uses the rubric and gated relations to return localized textual gradients: for example, "qualify the claim in span $i$ because it conflicts with span $j$." The editor applies those instructions to obtain a revised document, which can then be rescored. This textual-gradient loop is related to [[ProTeGi: Prompt Optimization with Textual Gradients]].

Discrete energy model and pairwise aggregation

Let $x$ be a token sequence, $a$ a rubric or attention vector over its spans, and $w$ the task context. Since text is discrete, the partition function is a sum over sequences (or over a finite candidate space), not an integral: $p_\theta(x\mid a,w)=\exp(-E_\theta(x,a,w))/Z_\theta(a,w)$, with $Z_\theta(a,w)=\sum_x \exp(-E_\theta(x,a,w))$. Lower energy means a better rubric fit. This is the normalized Boltzmann model used in [[Concept Learning with Energy-Based Models]].

For span $i$, let $u_\theta(x_i,w)$ be its unary energy, $s_i=\sigma(a_i)$ its attention gate, and let $g_\theta(x_i,x_j,w)$ be a compatibility logit, where larger values mean more compatible spans. Define $h_{ij}=s_i s_j$ and the pair penalty $c_{ij}=\text{softplus}(-g_\theta(x_i,x_j,w))$. The explicit aggregate is $E_\theta(x,a,w)=\sum_i s_i u_\theta(x_i,w)+\lambda\sum_{i<j}h_{ij} c_{ij}$. Thus attended spans contribute unary costs, while a pair contributes in proportion to both gates and incurs a high cost when its compatibility logit is low. This gated pairwise construction makes the relational aggregation explicit and connects energy-based composition to [[A Simple Neural Network Module for Relational Reasoning]]. The energy can be augmented with rubric-specific terms, but its components and weights should be reported so the critic can identify which terms drive a proposed edit.

Mixture importance sampling and softplus contrastive loss

Use a proposal mixture $q(x)=\sum_m \rho_m q_m(x)$, where $\rho_m\ge 0$ and $\sum_m\rho_m=1$. The mixture should cover plausible drafts and have support wherever the target has support. For samples $x_r$ drawn from $q$, the importance estimate is $\hat{Z}_\theta=\frac{1}{M}\sum_r \exp(-E_\theta(x_r,a,w))/q(x_r)$. The corresponding normalized-model score is approximated by $\ell_\theta(x)=\log[p_\theta(x\mid a,w)/q(x)]\approx -E_\theta(x,a,w)-\log \hat{Z}_\theta(a,w)-\log q(x)$.

To obtain the softplus objective, classify examples as either data or proposal draws with equal class priors. The logit for the data class against the proposal class is $\ell_\theta(x)$. Binary logistic negative log-likelihood is $\text{softplus}(-\ell_\theta(x))$ for a data example and $\text{softplus}(\ell_\theta(x))$ for a proposal example. Hence $\mathcal{L}^{\text{ML}}=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\text{data}}}[\text{softplus}(-\ell_\theta(x))]+\mathbb{E}_{x\sim q}[\text{softplus}(\ell_\theta(x))]$. This is an importance-corrected noise-contrastive objective with an importance-sampled, plug-in estimate of the partition function; it is approximate at finite $M$. In particular, estimate $Z$ with the full mixture density $q(x)$, not merely the component that happened to generate a sample. Monitor effective sample size and proposal coverage, since a poor mixture makes both $\hat{Z}$ and the resulting contrastive gradients unreliable. The contrastive perspective is related to [[Training Products of Experts by Minimizing Contrastive Divergence]]; importance-weighted proposals and energy-guided sampling also connect to [[Guided Cost Learning: Deep Inverse Optimal Control]].

From a critic's textual gradient to a discrete edit

A token sequence has no ordinary Euclidean gradient. Use a differentiable relaxed representation $z=\phi(x)$ for scoring and sampler training, and treat the resulting direction as guidance rather than as an exact gradient on strings. At each iteration: (1) score the current draft and spans under the rubric; (2) return localized instructions, such as qualify span $i$ because it conflicts with span $j$, or replace a phrase with a supported alternative; (3) have an editor propose a finite set of token or span edits; (4) estimate each proposal's energy change $\Delta E$ and rank or sample edits, for example with probability proportional to $\exp(-\Delta E/T)$, while retaining a nonzero exploration probability; and (5) apply a selected edit, rescore, and keep an audit trail of the instruction, edit, and energy change.

In the relaxed sampler, the update is $z^{k+1}=z^k-(\alpha/2)\nabla_z E_{\text{comp}}(z^k,a,w)+\omega^k$, followed by projection or decoding to a valid text $x^{k+1}=\text{ProjectText}(z^{k+1})$. Here $\omega^k$ is the exploration perturbation; the projected discrete process is an approximation, not exact Langevin dynamics on token strings. Gradients through stochastic edit rollouts can be estimated with a differentiable relaxation such as a soft token distribution, or with a score-function estimator for sampled edits. This procedure makes the textual-gradient step concrete while extending the localized edit loop of [[ProTeGi: Prompt Optimization with Textual Gradients]] and the feedback-as-differentiation framing of [[TextGrad: Automatic Differentiation via Text]].

Resolving the short-chain K=10 failure with rollout KL shaping

Lab Notebook #42's failure mode is that ten unshaped transitions can remain close to their initial drafts, so critiques from those samples underrepresent useful alternatives. Shape the sampler before using its outputs for critique. Let $\tau=(x^0,e_0,x^1,\dots,e_{K-1},x^K)$ be an edit rollout drawn from differentiable sampler $Q_\phi(\tau\mid x^0)$, and define a broad reference edit policy $Q_0$. Choose the stated target path distribution $P^*(\tau\mid x^0)$ proportional to $Q_0(\tau\mid x^0)\exp(-\beta E_\theta(x^K,a,w))$; $\beta$ controls energy guidance. Minimize $\mathcal{L}^{\text{KL}}=\text{KL}(Q_\phi\parallel P^*)$. Up to a constant independent of $\phi$, this is $\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim Q_\phi}[\beta E_\theta(x^K,a,w)+\log(Q_\phi(\tau\mid x^0)/Q_0(\tau\mid x^0))]$. The energy term steers terminal drafts toward rubric-compatible low-energy regions; the log-ratio term regularizes the edit policy relative to the reference. Differentiate through relaxed edit probabilities, or use a policy-gradient estimator for discrete edits.

Operationally, train or update $Q_\phi$ against this target, draw $K=10$ rollouts from the shaped sampler, then send their terminal drafts to the critic and apply the localized edit procedure above. This directly addresses the diagnosed short-chain problem by steering the finite-horizon rollout rather than assuming ten ordinary Langevin steps have mixed. A broad $Q_0$ and moderate $\beta$ preserve more variation; an overly concentrated target or an overly strong energy term can collapse diversity. Track distinct edits, coverage, energy, and effective sample size, and adjust $\beta$ or mix shaped rollouts with reference-policy rollouts if coverage falls. KL shaping improves the specified finite-horizon sampling objective; it does not guarantee exact equilibrium samples or eliminate mode loss. This sampler-and-energy viewpoint is connected to [[Bayesian Learning via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics]], [[Guided Cost Learning: Deep Inverse Optimal Control]], and [[Lab Notebook #42: Why Short-Run K=10 Langevin Chains Fail Without KL Shaping]].
