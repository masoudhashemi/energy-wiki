## Energy-based modeling and sampling

Energy functions score candidate states or trajectories; a Boltzmann distribution assigns probability proportional to exp(-E)/Z, with lower-energy states more probable. Since the partition function Z can be intractable, methods approximate its contribution: contrastive divergence compares data energy with energy after a short MCMC or Langevin chain [[D08]], while guided cost learning uses importance sampling and a KL objective to encourage low-energy samples [[D05]].

The short-chain premise and its limitations deserve explicit distinction. Classical contrastive divergence is described as assuming that truncated K-step Langevin chains mix sufficiently without differentiating through the sampler. In contrast, one experimental report finds that uncalibrated K=10 chains trained with L^ML alone fail to reach low-energy modes, and reports sampler-KL differentiation as necessary in that setting [[D03]]. A concept-learning model accordingly combines contrastive maximum likelihood with a sampler-KL loss differentiated through its K-step SGLD rollout [[D09]]. These finite-run observations do not negate the separate asymptotic result: calibrated-noise SGLD approaches the target posterior as step size tends to zero and iterations tend to infinity [[D16]]. Energy minimization also appears in few-shot concept acquisition [[D07]], and maximum-entropy IRL models demonstrated trajectories with Boltzmann distributions and describes additive composition of constraint energies [[D18]].

## Critique-driven textual gradients

Textual-gradient methods have a critic diagnose failures and provide natural-language guidance for iterative edits. ProTeGi applies critiques from demonstration minibatches to instruction optimization [[D10]], while TextGrad extends textual gradients to prompts, code, and wiki pages [[D17]]. A wiki-compilation note proposes pairwise relation critiques to detect missing cross-document links and add bidirectional wiki-links [[D04]]. Pairwise relational reasoning can represent interactions that independent unary encoders miss, though that source concerns relational modeling rather than textual-gradient optimization itself [[D11]].

Both concepts involve iterative optimization, but the optimized objects and mechanisms differ: energy-based methods score states and sample from a probability distribution; textual-gradient methods use language critiques to revise discrete content.
