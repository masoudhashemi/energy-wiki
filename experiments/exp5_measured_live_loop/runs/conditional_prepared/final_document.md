# Energy-Based Sampling and Critique-Guided Text Editing

## Energy scores and sampling

An energy function E(x,w) scores a state x under parameters or concept w; it does not, by itself, generate states. In a Boltzmann model, the score defines a probability distribution, p(x|w) = exp(-E(x,w))/Z(w). For a fixed model, lower-energy states receive greater probability, while the partition function Z normalizes the distribution. [[Maximum Entropy Inverse Reinforcement Learning]]

A sampler is a separate procedure for drawing states from that distribution. Hinton’s contrastive-divergence method uses a short MCMC or Langevin chain to produce negative samples and compares their energies with data states. [[Training Products of Experts by Minimizing Contrastive Divergence]] Welling and Teh describe SGLD, whose stated sampling limit is the vanishing-step-size, infinite-iteration regime: it adds calibrated noise to gradient updates and samples from the target distribution in that limit. [[Bayesian Learning via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics]] Mordatch et al. use **K=10 execution-time SGLD steps** to sample under an energy model for concept generation and entity identification. [[Concept Learning with Energy-Based Models]]

Guided Cost Learning approximates the partition function Z by importance sampling from a mixture of demonstrations and the sampling distribution pi. It minimizes KL(pi || exp(-E)/Z), training pi to generate low-energy negative samples. [[Guided Cost Learning: Deep Inverse Optimal Control]]

The contradiction note characterizes contrastive divergence as assuming that short chains mix sufficiently, then reports that its own K=10 chains fail to reach low-energy modes without a sampler-KL objective. [[Lab Notebook #42: Why Short-Run K=10 Langevin Chains Fail Without KL Shaping]] This is a report about finite-K behavior, not a refutation of SGLD’s stated infinite-iteration, vanishing-step-size limit; Hinton’s cited account says short chains are used but does not itself establish that they mix sufficiently. An energy score and a method for sampling from its distribution are distinct, and finite-run sampling may be inadequate.

## Critique-guided text editing

A separate application is iterative editing of discrete text. At execution time, a critic diagnoses failures and returns natural-language edit instructions; those instructions guide repeated revisions to a prompt or document. ProTeGi edits instructions based on failure minibatches. [[ProTeGi: Prompt Optimization with Textual Gradients]] TextGrad extends textual gradients to discrete variables such as prompts, code, and wiki pages, using a loss or energy rubric to guide successive edits. [[TextGrad: Automatic Differentiation via Text]] They are related forms of critique-guided iteration: ProTeGi grounds edits in diagnosed failures, while TextGrad frames feedback as rubric-guided textual gradients. Unlike TextGrad’s textual edits, MAML adapts parameters through execution-time gradient-descent steps and meta-learns parameters for that adaptation. [[Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning for Fast Adaptation (MAML)]]

For wiki compilation, the devlog reports a proposed pairwise editing approach to missing cross-document links: it gates relation critiques for entity pairs with the attention product σ(a_i)σ(a_j), then describes emitting LINK_DEFICIT textual gradients to create bidirectional wiki-links between co-attended entities during K-step execution-time adaptation. This is the devlog’s reported approach, not an independently established result. [[DevLog 2026-11-12: Redis Cache Timeout & Pairwise Wiki-Link Textual Gradients]] These textual critiques are distinct from Santoro et al.’s pairwise relational interactions, which compute relations between entity pairs in a neural network. [[A Simple Neural Network Module for Relational Reasoning]] Both approaches use pairwise context to represent cross-entity relationships that independent unary processing can miss, but this shared motivation does not make their procedures equivalent: the devlog describes a textual editing mechanism, whereas Santoro describes a neural-network method. Critique-guided edits are language-based guidance for discrete revisions—not numerical gradient updates or Langevin sampling.
