Rough initial idea: Maybe we can use energy-based models together with textual gradients to optimize text documents. Energy models assign low energy to good states. An energy function scores candidate states, and Boltzmann-based sampling explores candidates rather than only selecting the current best one, connecting this approach to [[Maximum Entropy Inverse Reinforcement Learning]]. More specifically, the energy function ranks candidate states, while MCMC sampling explores alternatives; this distinction connects to [[Guided Cost Learning: Deep Inverse Optimal Control]] and [[Training Products of Experts by Minimizing Contrastive Divergence]]. LLM critics can write critiques to improve a draft. A critic can diagnose a specific failure, propose a discrete edit, and then evaluate that edit, as in [[TextGrad: Automatic Differentiation via Text]] and [[ProTeGi: Prompt Optimization with Textual Gradients]]. Varied edits can help avoid local stagnation, but discrete edit diversity does not imply well-mixed Langevin chains; short-run chains can remain poorly mixed without KL shaping, as discussed in [[Lab Notebook #42: Why Short-Run K=10 Langevin Chains Fail Without KL Shaping]].
